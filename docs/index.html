How it plays

Live airflowDucts glow by air speed and a heatmap shows where the air actually reaches. Open ends leak into the attic.
Every part mattersSharp elbows and square tees lose air. Long-radius turns and tapered branches cost more but push more through.
Metal budgetEach house has a metal limit, so the best layout covers the most rooms with the least duct.
Bigger housesEarn from every house you've finished, buy upgrades, and move up from a cottage to a family home.

Getting started

1. Install
  1. Open Airflow.dmg and drag Airflow into Applications.
  2. Open Airflow. If macOS says it “could not verify” the app, click Done.
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to Airflow.
  4. Enter your Mac password and click Open Anyway again. You only need to do this once.
2. Build your first system
  1. Pick Galvanized Duct from the bar at the bottom and drag from the blue AHU into a room.
  2. Switch to Transition (4) and put a Register Boot on the end of the run. Press T to aim it.
  3. Use T-Branches to split air into other rooms. Cap any open end with an End Cap.
  4. Hover any part to see how much air goes through it. The Stats panel shows each room's coverage.
Controls
  • B build mode · R rotate · T aim register · F damper · Q pick part · X remove
  • Z view · drag to select · C/V copy and paste · ⌘Z undo
  • WASD move · right-drag to pan · scroll to zoom · 1–4 part categories · Tab panels
Updates and your save

Airflow checks for new versions a few seconds after it opens and every few hours. When one is ready, a banner at the top offers Install & Restart. Updates are signed, and Airflow refuses any that aren't. Your progress is kept on your Mac and carries over between versions.
